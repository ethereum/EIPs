## Abstract

This EIP defines `eth/73`, a version of the [`eth` wire protocol](https://github.com/ethereum/devp2p/blob/2c19a28b25c29487773ad6b07243e290fa8d65ec/caps/eth.md) that propagates [EIP-8288](./eip-8288.md) proof wrappers ("wrappers" below). Peers announce wrapper modes, lengths and hashes, then retrieve wrappers by hash.

## Motivation

EIP-8288 separates signatures and proofs from transaction envelopes and permits aggregation across transactions. Existing transaction propagation does not identify this shared proof material, so peers need a way to discover and retrieve it.

Accounts using other signature schemes also need to deliver raw witnesses to aggregators without first producing a STARK themselves. An optional raw-witness wrapper lets supporting peers collect signatures for a shared EIP-8288 batch program.

## Specification

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in [RFC 2119](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2119) and [RFC 8174](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8174).

`eth/73` extends `eth/72` ([EIP-8070](./eip-8070.md)), using its notation and message semantics except as specified below. Status (`0x00`) is unchanged; the protocol version and new message IDs are provisional. Request IDs follow [EIP-2481](./eip-2481.md). Clients SHOULD advertise `eth/73` once EIP-8288 is scheduled.

### New messages

Messages use canonical RLP with the structures below and no extra fields or trailing bytes.

Until EIP-8288 is enabled for local mempool validation, clients MUST NOT send these messages and MUST ignore them without penalties; underlying transport size limits still apply.

#### NewProofWrapperHashes (0x16)

`[[mode₀: P, length₀: P, hash₀: B_32, deps-hash₀: B_32, txs-hash₀: B_32], ...]`

Announce 1–64 held wrappers in strictly ascending hash order: mode is `0`, `1` or `2` (raw witnesses, below), length is 1–`MAX_WRAPPER_BYTES` as defined by [EIP-8288](./eip-8288.md#constants), and hash is Keccak-256 of the complete canonical RLP wrapper, including pooled transaction encodings. Peers SHOULD announce newly validated wrappers to every connected `eth/73` peer and, on connection, up to 64 offered wrappers covering at least one pending transaction, most recent first. Mode 2 announcements are subject to RawWitnessPolicy below.

`deps-hash` is Keccak-256 of the concatenated 96-byte entries in the canonical `deps` list; `txs-hash` is Keccak-256 of the concatenated, ascending consensus transaction hashes. Requesters MAY skip a fetch when both match a held wrapper validated under the current rules. Hints alone establish no coverage or validity; matching only `deps-hash` does not identify the transactions.

Mode 2 coverage MUST NOT suppress fetching or announcing a mode 0 or 1 wrapper. A validated proof wrapper may suppress equivalent raw coverage. Clients MAY ignore mode 2 announcements when they do not accept raw witnesses.

Under the same verification rules, peers SHOULD suppress hashes already exchanged on the connection and wrappers whose transactions are covered by held, validated wrappers exchanged with or announced by the recipient, unless they reduce proof bytes or estimated verification work, or provide an IL-bounded mode-1 proof where existing coverage cannot. Peers MAY re-announce a hash once a local interval has passed or it has left their bounded record of the connection.

The announcement tuple is fixed for `eth/73`. Extending it requires a new protocol version; canonical decoding rejects extra fields.

#### GetProofWrappers (0x17)

`[request-id: P, compact: P, [hash₀: B_32, hash₁: B_32, ...]]`

Request 1 to 32 distinct wrapper hashes per message, each previously announced by the recipient on that connection under the same verification rules. Responders MUST treat hashes they have not announced as unavailable and MUST NOT penalize the request, since either side's record of announcements may be bounded. `compact` is `0` or `1`: with `0`, returned wrappers MUST contain complete transaction and program encodings; with `1`, responders MAY use hash references as below.

#### ProofWrappers (0x18)

`[request-id: P, [[hash₀: B_32, wrapper₀], [hash₁: B_32, wrapper₁], ...]]`

Return available validated wrappers as embedded [EIP-8288 RLP items](./eip-8288.md#mempool-wrapper-object), paired with their canonical hashes, in request order with omissions allowed and no repeats. The recommended soft response limit is 2 MiB. Responders SHOULD stop after reaching it, returning complete wrappers only. Empty responses are permitted.

Requesters match distinct requested hashes in order, reconstruct wrappers as below, check canonical hashes, modes and full lengths against the recipient's announcements, and re-request hashes after the last returned wrapper. Earlier omissions are unavailable from that peer. Requesters SHOULD fetch each wrapper from one announcer at a time and try another after an empty response or timeout.

Responders whose serving budget is spent SHOULD return an empty response rather than omit wrappers or stay silent. Requesters SHOULD NOT penalize an empty response or count it toward a limit on sources tried for a wrapper. They MAY score earlier omissions in a nonempty response in proportion to the requested wrappers withheld, and SHOULD count an unanswered request against the peer. A future version may add an explicit retry-after hint.

#### Hash-based omission

- With `compact = 1`, responders MAY replace transaction or program bytes previously sent in full within a validated wrapper on that connection with `[encoding-hash: B_32]`, if smaller. The hash is Keccak-256 of those bytes, excluding the enclosing RLP string prefix. References are RLP lists; complete encodings are byte strings.
- Resolve references from cached, validated encodings and check their hashes. The complete wrapper determines identity and byte limits; reconstruction MUST stay within the announced length.
- On a missing encoding, requesters MUST discard the compact item and SHOULD retry with `compact = 0` and a fresh request ID. Cache misses MUST NOT incur penalties.

#### RawWitnessPolicy (0x19)

`[accept-new: P, [vm-version₀: P, vm-version₁: P, ...], [program-id₀: B_32, program-id₁: B_32, ...]]`

Advertise supported VM profiles and program policy. Both lists contain at most 64 entries in strictly ascending order; VM versions are unsigned 64-bit integers. `accept-new = 0` accepts only listed programs; `accept-new = 1` permits unfamiliar programs and requires an empty program list. Both cases require the program's VM version to be listed. An empty profile list permits no raw submissions.

Supporting peers SHOULD send their policy on connection and when it changes; each message replaces the previous policy. Before receiving one, assume no raw-witness support. Peers SHOULD announce mode 2 wrappers only when every program and its profile are permitted, including after a policy update permits previously unoffered wrappers. Policy is advisory: acceptance, relay and proof production remain optional and subject to local budgets; refusal MUST NOT incur penalties.

### Raw-witness wrappers

Mode `2` is a batching request, encoded as `[transactions, 2, [deps, witnesses, programs]]`. Transaction encoding, ordering, dependency union, identity, byte limits and compact retrieval are the same as for proof wrappers; EIP-8288's aggregate and per-transaction dependency bounds apply. There MUST be at least one `LEANSTARK_SCHEME` dependency and exactly one witness per dependency, in order:

- A leanSPHINCS witness is the direct signature byte string, subject to EIP-8288's mode 0 signature bound.
- A leanSTARK witness is `[format-id: B_32, witness: B]`. The dependency's `verification_key_hash` identifies the batch program; `format-id` selects its witness decoder and parameters, not a consensus signature scheme.

`programs` contains `[program-id: B_32, program: B]` entries in strictly ascending identifier order, exactly one for each distinct leanSTARK program ID in `deps`. Each program is an executable batch package defined by [EIP-8288](./eip-8288.md); all package bytes count toward `MAX_WRAPPER_BYTES`. Clients MUST match its domain-separated content hash to `program-id` before execution or reuse. This requires no proof-system preprocessing; a mismatch is a hash violation under Validation.

Before accepting or relaying mode 2, clients MUST validate its witnesses and check transactions under normal pool rules:

- Verify leanSPHINCS witnesses under EIP-8288. For each generic witness, execute the package's isolated `check(format-id, witness)` and require its 32-byte output to equal the resolved dependency's `data_hash`, after EIP-8288's zero-hash substitution.
- Execute supplied bytecode only in the profile's metered sandbox, without host I/O or supplied native code. Bound steps, memory and output, including accelerator work. A supported profile permits new programs without a per-scheme client adapter.
- Unknown profiles, unsupported programs and exhausted budgets MAY be declined without penalty. Witness rejection, including noncanonical encodings rejected by a program, is a validation failure, not malformed transport. Program authors should specify accepted encodings and provide conformance vectors for each format.

Accepted transactions enter a bounded batching queue. They MUST NOT be treated as proof-backed pool entries, used for inclusion-list obligations, or included in a block until a valid EIP-8288 aggregate proof covers their dependencies. Before constructing a leaf, aggregators MUST check records using the same isolated program semantics, removing rejected witnesses and their dependent transactions from the queue and relay set. If combined work exceeds a batch budget, split or defer the batch; otherwise valid records remain valid. The resulting leaf is incorporated into a mode 1 wrapper.

Clients MUST evict queued transactions once expired under [EIP-8141's mempool rules](./eip-8141.md#mempool) and SHOULD deprioritize work unlikely to finish before expiry. No proof-production deadline is guaranteed. While a transaction remains valid, senders MAY try another advertising peer or re-announce under the existing local-interval rule. Gas is unchanged by raw submission or batch size.

Mode 2 uses the existing per-peer and pool budgets, including program, witness and queued transaction bytes and execution/proving work; clients SHOULD bound this queue separately so raw submissions cannot evict proof-backed entries. For the relay rule below, acceptance into this queue counts as admission. Blob cell retrieval and provider announcements still require a proof wrapper.

### Transaction propagation

Dependency-bearing transactions MUST NOT appear in Transactions (`0x02`) or PooledTransactions (`0x0a`) on any version; GetPooledTransactions (`0x09`) treats them as unavailable. NewPooledTransactionHashes (`0x08`) MUST NOT announce them except for blob availability below.

#### Blob-carrying dependency transactions

On `eth/73`, EIP-8070 applies to blob-carrying frame transactions. ProofWrappers carries [EIP-8141's pooled network form](./eip-8141.md#networking), with blob data elided as specified by [EIP-8070](./eip-8070.md#execution-clients--devp2p-changes).

- After wrapper validation and EIP-8070's checks, clients SHOULD advertise cells through NewPooledTransactionHashes, with covering-wrapper announcements subject to suppression. Unknown frame hashes may elicit empty GetPooledTransactions responses.
- GetCells/Cells use consensus transaction hashes. Dependency-bearing transactions require a validated covering wrapper before cell retrieval and EIP-8070's checks before admission. Validated wrappers may circulate before availability is established; their announcements do not count as providers.

### Validation

- Clients MUST check the complete wrapper's encoding, length and hash and validate under EIP-8288 (or the mode 2 rules above) before announcement, serving, reaggregation or transaction admission. Until validation completes, received wrapper data MUST remain in bounded request-scoped storage and be discarded on cancellation or timeout.

- Validate using the EIP-8288 fork rules selected for local mempool validation. If those rules change, for example through a new proof verifier or dependency limits, wrappers validated only under the previous rules MUST NOT be served, announced or used to suppress announcements.

- Before decoding or hashing wrapper bodies, requesters SHOULD check that the response's request ID is outstanding and its connection open; responders SHOULD skip requests from closed connections.

- Transport item-count, hard byte-length and response-order violations SHOULD cause disconnection. Malformed transport encodings, unmatched or excess responses, hash, mode or length mismatches, and references in a `compact = 0` response MUST be discarded and MAY cause penalties or disconnection. Crossing the soft response limit alone MUST NOT cause penalties. A complete wrapper that is not canonical EIP-8288 RLP is likewise malformed.

- Once the canonical wrapper is reconstructed, clients MUST check its announcement digests alongside its hash, before proof verification. A mismatch MUST cause discard and SHOULD cause disconnection, independently of proof validity.

- Pool rejection of an individual transaction does not invalidate its covering wrapper and MUST NOT incur peer penalties. Clients SHOULD relay such a wrapper unchanged only if it newly admitted at least one transaction and no fewer than it rejected; already-pending transactions count as neither.

- Proof, raw-witness or dependency-rule failures, including exceeding `MAX_SIGS_PER_TX` or another EIP-8288 dependency limit, MUST cause the wrapper to be discarded without peer penalties or disconnection for that failure. Such wrappers are invalid, not malformed: only failures of the transport, encoding, hash, mode, length and digest checks above are malformed.

- Clients MAY deprioritize or rate-limit fetching from peers whose wrappers repeatedly fail validation or whose requests go unanswered, MAY disconnect such peers past a local threshold, and SHOULD retain this standing by node identity across reconnections.

- Clients SHOULD bound served ProofWrappers bytes globally and divide the budget fairly between peers, for example 32 MiB/s in total and at most 8 MiB/s per peer.

## Rationale

Wrappers carry transactions alongside their dependency proofs so receivers can validate them together. Separate propagation would require additional mempool state for envelopes awaiting coverage, or fetching those envelopes again after the proof arrives.

Reusing EIP-8070's cell announcements preserves its provider and sampler behavior. Putting cell masks in wrappers would make wrapper identity depend on each peer's changing holdings.

Mode distinguishes direct-witness verification from recursive-proof verification; length gives the complete wrapper's encoded size. Dependency and transaction digests identify equivalent coverage despite different proof bytes, avoiding redundant downloads. Compared with a dependency hint alone, `txs-hash` adds 33 encoded bytes per announcement to distinguish different transactions sharing dependencies. The wire dependency digest uses a fixed hash of encoded triples rather than the circuit digest, allowing checks independent of fork verification rules.

Mode 2 carries witnesses awaiting a batch proof; native-only submissions already use mode 0. Executing the pinned program's isolated check avoids divergence between native signature adapters and proving semantics. One program may support several schemes. Supplying each program once per wrapper permits first-contact validation without a separate retrieval protocol; compact responses reuse cached packages. RawWitnessPolicy exposes neighboring peers' willingness to handle new programs without changing Status or guaranteeing a prover.

Hash omission saves repeated transaction and program bytes while preserving canonical identity, at the cost of cache tracking and full retries after eviction. Encoding hashes also bind blob metadata.

## Backwards Compatibility

`eth/73` may be negotiated before EIP-8288 activates. Older versions continue exchanging blocks and other transactions but receive neither wrappers nor dependency-bearing envelopes through transaction messages.

## Test Cases

[Announcement vectors](../assets/eip-8437/announcement-vectors.json), [wrapper vectors](../assets/eip-8437/wrapper-vectors.json) and [the checker](../assets/eip-8437/check_vectors.py) cover the following encoding and matching cases; synthetic envelopes and witnesses do not test transaction or proof validity.

| Checker case | Expected result |
| --- | --- |
| Maximum announcement/request; excess count | 6,787/1,072 encoded bytes for 64 announcements/32 hashes; reject excess |
| Request a received announcement; request an unannounced hash | Requester selects the former; responder omits the latter |
| Changed enclosed envelope, proof bytes or announced mode/length | Reject mismatch |
| Different proof bytes, identical dependency and transaction sets | Different wrapper hashes; equal coverage hints permit skipping the fetch |
| Equal dependency digest, different transaction sets | Transaction digests differ; dependency equality alone does not suppress fetching |
| False dependency or transaction digest, including with an invalid proof | Reject mismatch before proof verification |
| Empty envelopes, unresolved references in a canonical wrapper, duplicate or reordered envelopes | Reject malformed or noncanonical wrapper |
| Full response to either request flag, without omission caches | Accept |
| Compact response with cached references; missing reference | Reconstruct the identical canonical wrapper; discard the incomplete item and retry in full |
| Reference when `compact = 0`, wrong cached encoding or false canonical hash | Reject |
| Compact form fits the byte cap but reconstructed wrapper exceeds it | Reject |
| Mode 2 with ordered witnesses and one package per program; wrong program hash, missing or duplicate package, witness or malformed format ID | Accept the encoding; reject the malformed cases |
| Equal hints for raw and proven wrappers | Raw coverage cannot suppress the proof; proven coverage may suppress the raw wrapper |
| Compact program reference, cache miss and full retry | Preserve full-object identity; use the existing retry path; reject a reference with `compact = 0` |
| RawWitnessPolicy with explicit programs, wildcard or no policy | Require every program and its profile to be permitted; reject excess, duplicate or unordered entries |
| Wildcard or explicit program with an unlisted VM profile | Do not announce; listing a program does not override the profile list |
| Blob network wrapper with elided data, commitments and cell proofs | Accept encoding; use the consensus transaction hash for ordering and cell requests |
| Nonempty blob data, mismatched commitments or wrong proof counts | Reject wrapper; metadata changes also change wrapper identity |
| Complete wrapper exactly at 2 MiB; one byte larger | Accept the size boundary, including response overhead; reject the oversized wrapper |
| Response crosses soft limit on final wrapper | Stop packing; receiver accepts overshoot |
| Response omits whole wrappers, repeats or reorders them | Accept omissions; reject repeats/reordering |

Client integration cases require peer and fork state not modeled by the checker:

- Suppress wrappers covering already-covered transactions unless cheaper or newly IL-reusable.
- Hints never establish coverage before validation. A correct hint remains consistent across verifier changes; an incorrect hint is penalizable independently of proof failure.
- Before local activation, ignore wrapper messages without penalties and send none.
- Discard a wrapper failing proof or dependency validation, including one exceeding `MAX_SIGS_PER_TX`, without penalizing its sender, including across a verifier change; malformed transport or mismatched announced hashes remain penalizable.
- Omit an encoding previously sent in full on the connection; retain its cache entry for reconstruction or retry in full after eviction. Never omit an encoding not previously delivered.
- A valid wrapper remains relayable unchanged when one transaction fails pool admission; that rejection does not penalize the sender.
- A complete wrapper can be validated without envelope requests; cancelled or timed-out validation releases its request buffer without admitting transactions.
- An unknown blob frame hash triggers an empty GetPooledTransactions response; its covering wrapper later arrives. Fetch cells only after validating it; admit only after EIP-8070's checks.
- Multiple wrappers covering A do not add providers or reset sampling. Two peers' full-cell announcements count as two providers; invalid cells do not invalidate the dependency proof or unrelated transactions.
- Authenticate a previously unknown program by hashing its package, execute it within the supported profile's sandbox, and compare its claim with the resolved dependency. Mismatched program hashes are rejected before execution; unsupported profiles and exhausted budgets may be declined without penalties.
- A generic dependency declared with zero resolves to the transaction's signature hash before raw validation. Its program authenticates the transaction's credential and policy and emits that hash; an arbitrary explicit claim is compared without substitution.
- Singleton and batch construction execute the same isolated routine. Rejecting witnesses are removed without discarding unrelated valid queue entries; combined budget exhaustion triggers splitting or deferral. Program decoder rejection is not malformed transport.
- A validated mode 2 wrapper remains only in the bounded batching queue until a valid proof covers it. Evict at EIP-8141 expiry, not while the expiry timestamp still equals the local head timestamp. Expiry or a full queue does not establish misbehavior; the later mode 1 wrapper remains fetchable despite identical coverage hints.
- Batch two alternative-signature transactions, including different schemes supported by one program, into one leaf. Only the resulting mode 1 wrapper establishes proof coverage for ordinary pool admission or inclusion lists.

## Security Considerations

Clients should bound per-peer announcements and policy updates, outstanding requests, buffered and cached bytes, verification/proving work and serving traffic, including mode 2 queues. Untrusted programs require authenticated bytecode, sandbox isolation and metering; interpreter costs require benchmarks. Package authentication is hashing work bounded by wrapper bytes. A program accepting meaningless claims weakens accounts that pin it but still consumes peers' bounded resources. Proof availability and batch fill add latency; peer policy does not guarantee propagation or proving. Request deadlines limit withholding; large responses delay other messages. Validation before relay adds per-hop download and verification latency; propagation within the aggregation interval requires measurements. EIP-8070's blobpool safeguards apply. Empty responses, responses cut at the soft limit and local budget exhaustion alone should not incur penalties.
