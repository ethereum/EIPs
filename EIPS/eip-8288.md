## Abstract

This EIP extends [EIP-8141](./eip-8141.md) with a new frame mode that enables EVM-level aggregation of cryptographic signatures and STARK (Scalable Transparent ARguments of Knowledge) proofs. The scheme allows transactions to carry lightweight **dependencies** — `(scheme, data_hash, verification_key_hash)` triples — which are aggregated off-chain into a single recursive STARK. Block validity requires this recursive STARK, enabling efficient post-quantum (PQ) and scalable signature aggregation using Lean Ethereum tooling.

## Motivation

Quantum-resistant signatures and zero-knowledge proofs both have a very high calldata requirement and gas cost. Current proposals for hash-based signatures are in the range of ~2-3 kB and ~150,000 to 200,000 gas to verify. Lattice-based signatures have a similar cost. Meanwhile, hash-based zero-knowledge proofs (STARKs) all take over 128 kB, or as much as 512 kB if fast generation is a goal (which it is client-side), which implies a STARK will take many millions of gas to verify. This on its own would mean that privacy protocols will have an impractically high cost, and be completely incompatible with FOCIL ([EIP-7805](./eip-7805.md)) and Frame Transactions ([EIP-8141](./eip-8141.md)).

This EIP solves this problem by adding **STARK-based aggregation**. Instead of a block directly containing these user-submitted signatures and STARKs, the block contains one single recursive STARK that proves their existence. More specifically, it:

- Introduces **dependency frames** that declare `(scheme, data_hash, verification_key_hash)` triples as transaction-level dependencies.
- Requires a **recursive STARK** at the block level that proves the validity of all dependencies in all transactions.
- Reuses **Lean Ethereum** tooling (for signatures and STARKs), to maximize shared codebase.
- Provides a **mempool wrapper** format that bundles transactions with their dependencies and proofs.
- Supports **recursive aggregation** at the mempool layer, allowing mempool nodes to combine proofs and reduce bandwidth overhead.
- Is **compatible with FOCIL** ([EIP-7805](./eip-7805.md)), ensuring that the aggregation scheme works with fork-choice enforced inclusion lists.

### Cryptographic parameters

`MAX_AGGREGATE_PROOF_BYTES` is provisional pending worst-case proof-size, verifier and transport benchmarks, including committee-wide inclusion-list proof data. Its headroom admits other provers and proof systems, and stronger security parameters such as higher query counts, whose proofs are larger. Whatever proof-dissemination window the consensus layer accepts, EIP-7805's `IL_COMMITTEE_SIZE` × `MAX_AGGREGATE_PROOF_BYTES` (20 MiB at 1.25 MiB) must fit it with margin; the proof-size benchmark must land well below that, or the committee's proof budget needs its own cap below the wrapper proof bound. The activating fork must fix this bound with its proof format and verifier, and it must fit a mode-1 wrapper at the list ceilings within `MAX_WRAPPER_BYTES` (see [Mempool-Level Limits](#mempool-level-limits)). One bound serves blocks, wrappers and inclusion lists because their proofs come from the one `AGGREGATED_VK` circuit and feed each other as recursive inputs.

The activating fork must fix `DEPS_HASH`, canonical aggregate proof and verification-key encodings, `AGGREGATED_VK`, and the verifier's cryptographic parameters and resource bounds. Every serialized recursive aggregate proof, including a private recursive input, must be at most `MAX_AGGREGATE_PROOF_BYTES` bytes. A longer proof is invalid, in blocks, wrappers and inclusion lists alike, not merely locally refused; reject it before proof decoding or verification. Block-body and Engine API encodings must carry proofs up to this bound. The header carries only the proof hash and dependency digest, regardless of proof size. A canonical `AGGREGATED_VK` identifies one immutable verifier. `DEPS_HASH` returns 32 bytes. The circuit identified by `AGGREGATED_VK` must enforce the exact `get_deps_hash` encoding below as its public dependency digest, including for leanSPHINCS-only sets.

`ENABLED_SCHEMES` is a per-fork constant, and the circuit identified by `AGGREGATED_VK` must accept exactly that set. Changing it or a scheme's verification relation requires a corresponding circuit and `AGGREGATED_VK` update, so forks sharing a key cannot differ in scheme sets. `ENABLED_SCHEMES` contains `LEANSPHINCS_SCHEME`. A fork may add `LEANSTARK_SCHEME` only with complete leanSTARK verification parameters specifying:

- canonical verification-key encoding for the key commitment defined below;
- canonical witness encoding carrying the verification key and proof, with key, proof and witness size limits;
- the proof format, verifier parameters and encoding of `data_hash` as the single 32-byte public statement;
- per-verification resource limits and the identical verification relation for direct witnesses and circuit execution.

The key-hash rule is `vk_hash = keccak256(domain_tag || canonical_vk)`, where `domain_tag` is the ASCII string `ethereum/8288/leanSTARK/vk/v1` followed by one zero byte. The domain tag prevents reuse of a key commitment across protocols and purposes; scheme separation is already provided by the dependency encoding. Keccak lets contracts and wallets derive `vk_hash` with existing EVM primitives; its in-circuit cost is bounded by the key size limit.

With those parameters, `verify_dependency_stark(data_hash, vk_hash, witness)` decodes the witness, checks that the canonical key hashes to `vk_hash`, and verifies the proof for exactly `data_hash`. Noncanonical encodings, mismatched keys or statements, and exceeded limits fail verification. If `LEANSTARK_SCHEME` is absent from `ENABLED_SCHEMES`, `0x11` dependencies are invalid. This gate does not disable recursive aggregate proofs, which use the fixed `AGGREGATED_VK`.

The aggregate circuit must also bound, per invocation, the counts and encoded sizes of direct witnesses, recursive proofs and their dependency lists, and discards, and the total verification work over them. These bounds include inputs whose dependencies are later discarded; limits on the final dependency set do not replace them.

For builder liveness under [EIP-7805](./eip-7805.md), a builder can maintain a running aggregate ahead of the slot. Its scope is the builder's validated mempool wrappers: it proves the union of their `deps`, which differs from `dependencies(block)`, since it includes dependencies of transactions the block will omit and lacks those of inclusion-list transactions the builder holds no wrapper for. Inclusion-list commitments can name wrapper proofs already folded into it (see [Extended FOCIL Data Structure](#extended-focil-data-structure)). On the critical path the builder merges the running aggregate with the committed proofs it does not cover and discards every dependency outside `dependencies(block)`: the running aggregate's surplus and the committed proofs' surplus, including dependencies of transactions the builder legitimately cannot include, such as ones invalid at their index or exceeding the remaining gas. The builder never strictly needs a committed proof: any proofs covering `dependencies(block)` suffice. How the merge is split into steps is a parameter of the benchmarked construction.

The activated proof format and verifier must support every dependency set permitted by the activating fork's transaction and block gas rules; wrapper limits do not apply to block proofs. The aggregation requirement is timeliness, not a particular architecture: the activating fork must specify a reference construction, in any number of bounded aggregation steps, and benchmark that it produces the block proof from the builder's inputs within the builder's proving budget, the time between the builder's last input and the block's proposal deadline, covering every step and every input, including overlapping dependencies and dependencies the construction discards. Builder liveness depends on the latency of one aggregation step times the number of steps that run in sequence. Per-step fan-in, discard counts, step counts and work done ahead of the slot are parameters of the benchmarked construction, not circuit requirements; any construction that meets the deadline on the benchmarked inputs conforms.

Each dependency costs `LEANSPHINCS_VERIFICATION_GAS` or `LEANSTARK_VERIFICATION_GAS` in its frame plus `LEANSTARK_VERIFICATION_GAS` of `recursive_stark_gas`. Gas therefore bounds the block's output, `dependencies(block)`, to fewer than `gas_limit / (LEANSPHINCS_VERIFICATION_GAS + LEANSTARK_VERIFICATION_GAS)` leanSPHINCS dependencies, and with `LEANSTARK_SCHEME` enabled fewer than `gas_limit / (2 * LEANSTARK_VERIFICATION_GAS)` leanSTARK dependencies, with any mix within the gas limit; the gas-derived count rounds these bounds up. Gas does not bound the construction's inputs, whose proofs can also cover dependencies of transactions the block omits. The required benchmark measures the time from the builder's last input to the finished block proof in two cases, at the activation gas limit with headroom and again before any gas-limit increase: the gas-derived case, a block proof over the gas-derived count from a larger running aggregate, and the cold inclusion-list case, which adds `IL_COMMITTEE_SIZE` committed proofs the builder does not hold, fetched after the view freeze deadline, and discards most of their dependencies. The running aggregate's size is the builder's choice, so the benchmark fixes it as a parameter and a builder keeps its running aggregate within what the benchmarked construction proves in time; workloads and provisional parameters are in the [benchmark protocol](../assets/eip-8288/benchmarks.md), and valid-block fixtures in [Test Case 11](#test-case-11-focil-compatibility). There is no per-block dependency cap, and inclusion-list obligations can push a builder toward either case, so both benchmarks are liveness preconditions, not advisory: a builder that cannot prove in time misses the slot, as with a block too slow to execute.

Attesters need their own benchmark; builder benchmark success alone does not establish it. On reference hardware, under the full permitted background load, an attester must receive on the proof topic (see [Proof Availability](#proof-availability)) and verify the committee's wrapper proofs published by the `t=0s` target of slot `N` (provisional), up to `IL_COMMITTEE_SIZE` × `MAX_AGGREGATE_PROOF_BYTES` (20 MiB) of proof data plus their dependency lists, and check each list committing to them or to held wrapper proofs with a cache lookup by the EIP-7805 view freeze deadline. This delivery is an activation requirement, without which the null fallback preserves the lists' dependency-free obligations but may substantially weaken censorship resistance for dependency-bearing transactions; late proofs are measured, not required, and the workloads, background load and reported fields are in the [benchmark protocol](../assets/eip-8288/benchmarks.md).

### New Frame Mode

This EIP introduces a new frame mode for [EIP-8141](./eip-8141.md) frame transactions:

#### Dependency Verification Frame (`DEP_VERIFY_FRAME_MODE`)

We modify EIP-8141 to also allow frames with mode `DEP_VERIFY_FRAME_MODE`, in addition to all previously allowed modes. `DEP_VERIFY_FRAME_MODE` is added to the set of mode values accepted by EIP-8141's static constraint on `frame.mode`; no other mode value becomes valid. A frame with mode `DEP_VERIFY_FRAME_MODE` is a **dependency verification frame**. It declares a set of **dependencies** as `(scheme, data_hash, verification_key_hash)` triples. These triples are not executed as EVM code; instead, they are recorded as dependencies that must be proven valid by the recursive STARK in the block.

The **payload encoding** is a simple `96*k` byte concatenation of `k` triples, each consisting of:

- `scheme`: 31 zero bytes padding, then 1 byte identifying the verification scheme (`LEANSPHINCS_SCHEME` or `LEANSTARK_SCHEME`).
- `data_hash`: 32 bytes. For `LEANSPHINCS_SCHEME`, this is the message hash (`msghash`). For `LEANSTARK_SCHEME`, this is the proof's single 32-byte public statement (`pub_input_hash`), encoded as specified by the activated leanSTARK verification parameters.
- `verification_key_hash`: 32 bytes. For `LEANSPHINCS_SCHEME`, this is the leanSPHINCS public key itself, laid out as `root (16 bytes) || public parameter (16 bytes)`, the order the aggregation circuit hashes it in; a key that already fits in 32 bytes needs no hash. For `LEANSTARK_SCHEME`, this is a hash of the STARK verification key (`vk_hash`).

This data is stored in the frame's `data` field.

**Receipt**:

A dependency verification frame produces an [EIP-8141](./eip-8141.md) frame receipt like any other frame, with `status = 1`, `gas_used.execution` equal to the frame's `limits.execution`, `gas_used.state = 0`, and no logs.

**Validity constraints**:

- The frame's `data` must be exactly `96*k` bytes, and the first 31 bytes of each set of 96 must be zero.
- For each set of 96 bytes, `scheme` (byte 31) must belong to the applicable fork's `ENABLED_SCHEMES`; all other values are invalid.
- Number of triples must be `>= 1` and `<= MAX_DEPENDENCIES_PER_FRAME`.
- The frame's `target` must be `None`. This is EIP-8141's absent target, not the zero address: `resolved_target` is then `tx.sender`, and `FRAMEPARAM(0x00, i)` returns the sender's address. Contracts identify dependency verification frames by `mode`, not by target.
- The frame's `value` must be `0`.
- The frame's `flags` must be `0`.
- The frame must not be part of an [EIP-8141](./eip-8141.md) atomic batch. Its `flags` are `0`, so this means the preceding frame, if any, must not carry `ATOMIC_BATCH_FLAG`.
- The frame's `limits.execution` must be `sum(LEANSPHINCS_VERIFICATION_GAS if triple.scheme == LEANSPHINCS_SCHEME else LEANSTARK_VERIFICATION_GAS for triple in frame.data_triples)`.
- The frame's `limits.state` must be `0`. The frame executes no code, so it can consume no state gas.

### Transaction Dependencies

A frame transaction may contain frames with mode `DEP_VERIFY_FRAME_MODE`. These frames declare **dependencies** that must be satisfied by the block's recursive STARK.

The complete list of dependencies for a transaction is the concatenation of all dependency triples from all dependency verification frames. The `data_triples` property of a frame is the list of `(scheme, data_hash, verification_key_hash)` triples parsed from the frame's `data` field:

```
frame.data_triples = [
    (data[31], data[32:64], data[64:96])
    for data in chunks(frame.data, 96)
]

dependencies(tx) = deduplicate_and_sort([
    (scheme, compute_sig_hash(tx) if data_hash == bytes(32) else data_hash, verification_key_hash)
    for frame in tx.frames if frame.mode == DEP_VERIFY_FRAME_MODE
    for (scheme, data_hash, verification_key_hash) in frame.data_triples
])

dependencies(block) = deduplicate_and_sort([
    triple
    for tx in block.txs
    for triple in dependencies(tx)
])
```

Where `deduplicate_and_sort` deduplicates and sorts:

```python
def deduplicate_and_sort(deps):
    o = []
    for dep in sorted(deps):
      if len(o) == 0 or dep != o[-1]:
        o.append(dep)
    return o
```

A `data_hash` of 32 zero bytes stands for the signature hash of the containing transaction, `compute_sig_hash(tx)` as defined in EIP-8141. A transaction cannot carry its own signature hash in a frame, because that hash covers the frame's `data`. `dependencies(tx)` holds the signature hash in place of the zero bytes, so the mempool wrapper, the FOCIL and the block prove a statement about that hash. The substitution applies to every scheme, so an all-zero `data_hash` is reserved: a leanSPHINCS message or leanSTARK `pub_input_hash` of zero cannot be declared.

The frame's `data` is unchanged: `compute_sig_hash` covers the zero bytes and `FRAMEDATACOPY` returns them, not the signature hash. A contract comparing a dependency with `TXPARAM(0x08)`, the transaction's signature hash, must treat a zero `data_hash` as that hash.

Let `input_deps` be the union of dependencies covered by all input proofs, including mode-0 direct witnesses, and `retained_deps` the dependency union of the selected transactions. The discard set is:

```python
def discard_dependencies(input_deps, retained_deps):
    inputs, retained = set(input_deps), set(retained_deps)
    assert retained <= inputs
    return deduplicate_and_sort(inputs - retained)
```

Wrapper pruning, FOCIL merging and block assembly use this rule. A dependency shared with any retained transaction is retained, even if another transaction declaring it is removed.

### Transaction Execution Visibility

During EVM execution, the transaction's dependencies are made available through the existing [EIP-8141](./eip-8141.md) `FRAMEPARAM` and `FRAMEDATACOPY` instructions.

Contracts can iterate over all frames in the transaction using `FRAMEPARAM` to identify `DEP_VERIFY_FRAME_MODE` frames, then use `FRAMEPARAM(0x04, i)` and `FRAMEDATACOPY` to read the dependency data from each frame's `data` field.

Specifically:

- `FRAMEPARAM(0x02, i)` returns the `mode` of frame `i`. Frames with mode `DEP_VERIFY_FRAME_MODE` are dependency frames.
- `FRAMEPARAM(0x04, i)` returns `len(data)` for frame `i`.
- `FRAMEDATACOPY(memOffset, dataOffset, length, i)` copies the dependency data from frame `i` into memory.

The data format for each dependency frame is a concatenation of 96-byte triples: `scheme || data_hash || verification_key_hash`.

This allows smart contracts to introspect the declared dependencies during execution using the existing EIP-8141 frame introspection mechanism.

### Block Validity: Recursive STARK

A block is valid only if it contains a **recursive STARK** that proves the validity of all dependencies in all transactions. The block header commits to the proof and the block body carries it. This is formalized as follows.

#### Recursive STARK Header Entry

A new top-level field, `recursive_stark`, is added to the block header. This field commits to the aggregated STARK proof for the block. The proof itself, `stark_proof`, is a new field of the block body, encoded after the existing fields, and a field of the execution payload. The `eth` `BlockBodies` encoding carries the new field from the activating fork, as for `withdrawals`.

**Header encoding**:

```
recursive_stark = [stark_proof_hash, block_deps_hash]
```

- `stark_proof`: The serialized recursive STARK proof, carried in the block body. It does not carry the dependencies or their keys; a verifier takes them from the block through `block_deps_hash`. If `dependencies(block)` is empty, `stark_proof` is empty.
- `stark_proof_hash`: `keccak256(stark_proof)`. A block whose `stark_proof` does not hash to this value is invalid.
- `block_deps_hash`: `get_deps_hash(dependencies(block))`, the digest the recursive STARK commits to in its public statement:

```
  get_deps_hash(deps) = DEPS_HASH(concat(encode(t) for t in deps))
```

`encode(t)` is the 96-byte payload encoding of [New Frame Mode](#new-frame-mode), `31 zero bytes || scheme || data_hash || verification_key_hash`, of each entry `t` of `dependencies(tx)` after zero-`data_hash` substitution, so digests never encode the zero placeholder. `get_deps_hash` takes a sorted, deduplicated list of dependencies whose schemes are enabled. Every entry uses this encoding, including the scheme; there is no 64-byte leanSPHINCS-only variant. The aggregation circuit commits to the same 96-byte encoding in its public statement. Clients and the aggregate circuit use this same function for block, wrapper, inclusion-list, recursive-input and discard-set commitments. A change to this encoding requires the corresponding circuit and `AGGREGATED_VK` to change together.

#### Recursive STARK Proof Structure

The recursive STARK is generated using Lean Ethereum tooling. It proves the following statement:

```python
public inputs = [deps_hash, AGGREGATED_VK]

private inputs = [deps, witnesses, recursive_proofs, recursive_deps_list, discards]

# get_deps_hash as defined for block_deps_hash
assert len(recursive_proofs) == len(recursive_deps_list)
assert all(
    dep[0] in ENABLED_SCHEMES
    for dep_list in [deps, discards] + recursive_deps_list
    for dep in dep_list
)

# Verify direct dependencies
assert len(deps) == len(witnesses)
for i in range(len(witnesses)):
    scheme, data_hash, verification_key_hash = deps[i]
    if scheme == LEANSPHINCS_SCHEME:
        assert verify_leanSPHINCS(data_hash, verification_key_hash, witnesses[i])
    else if scheme == LEANSTARK_SCHEME:
        assert verify_dependency_stark(data_hash, verification_key_hash, witnesses[i])
    else:
        assert False

# Verify recursive proofs and collect their deps
all_deps = list(deps)
for i in range(len(recursive_proofs)):
    proof = recursive_proofs[i]
    assert len(proof) <= MAX_AGGREGATE_PROOF_BYTES
    inner_deps = recursive_deps_list[i]
    # Verify the recursive proof's public input matches its claimed deps hash
    assert verify_leanSTARK((get_deps_hash(inner_deps), AGGREGATED_VK), AGGREGATED_VK, proof)
    # Add the inner deps to the union
    all_deps.extend(inner_deps)

# Remove only dependencies covered by verified inputs
assert set(discards) <= set(all_deps)
filtered_deps = deduplicate_and_sort(set(all_deps) - set(discards))

# Verify final deps hash matches public input
assert get_deps_hash(filtered_deps) == deps_hash
```

The recursive STARK circuit follows a "verify inputs, merge and discard" logic. It verifies:

- All leanSPHINCS signatures using the Lean Ethereum leanSPHINCS verifier
- All leanSTARKs using the Lean Ethereum leanSTARK verifier
- Recursive STARKs using the Lean Ethereum leanSTARK verifier.

For `LEANSPHINCS_SCHEME`, `verification_key_hash` carries the 32-byte public key directly. For `LEANSTARK_SCHEME`, the key commitment, witness and verification relation are defined by the activated leanSTARK verification parameters above. The circuit enforces its enabled-scheme and resource constraints on all private inputs, not only the dependencies retained in its output.

Every discard must occur in the verified input union. Discards remain subject to canonical encoding, enabled-scheme and private-input resource constraints, and use the set difference defined by `discard_dependencies`.

The recursive STARK circuit does not understand the structure of transactions or blocks beyond iterating over a flat list of `(scheme, data_hash, verification_key_hash)` triples, their corresponding witnesses, and nested recursive STARKs.

The recursive STARK is verified using a fixed protocol-level `AGGREGATED_VK`.

To enable unlimited-depth recursion, we need to pass the `AGGREGATED_VK` in as an input to the proof itself.

#### Block Validity Rules

The block validity conditions are extended as follows:

1. The `block_deps_hash` in the recursive STARK header entry equals `get_deps_hash(dependencies(block))`.
2. If `dependencies(block)` is empty, `stark_proof` is empty. Otherwise the recursive STARK proof verifies successfully against `block_deps_hash` and the fixed protocol-level `AGGREGATED_VK`.
3. All transactions satisfy the normal (EIP-8141 or other) validity rules.

### Mempool Wrapper Object

At the mempool level, transactions with dependency verification frames are wrapped in **wrapper objects** that are broadcast in bounded batches. Each wrapper contains a **list of transactions** and either their direct dependencies or a single recursive STARK proving all dependencies.

**Wrapper encoding**:

```
RLP([transactions, mode, [deps, proof_content]])
transactions = [transaction_hash, ...]
mode = 0 (direct dependencies) or 1 (recursive STARK)
deps = [dependency, ...]
proof_content = proofs        if mode == 0
proof_content = stark_proof   if mode == 1
```

Where:

- `transactions` = strictly ascending list of 32-byte `keccak256(transaction_envelope)` hashes, where each envelope is the exact [EIP-2718](./eip-2718.md) encoding of an EIP-8141 frame transaction. Envelopes are recovered through the wrapper transport; wrapper validity below refers to the resolved transactions. Dependency-bearing transactions are not exchanged in the `eth` wire protocol's transaction messages; in the mempool they propagate only through wrapper exchange.
- `deps` = sorted, deduplicated union of `dependencies(tx)` for all transactions in the wrapper, with the signature hash in place of each zero `data_hash`, each entry as its 96-byte payload encoding from [New Frame Mode](#new-frame-mode)
- `proofs` = list of individual proof byte strings corresponding to each dependency in `deps`, in order
- `stark_proof` = a single recursive STARK proving validity of all deps, with public input `get_deps_hash(deps)`

Every listed transaction must declare at least one dependency, so `deps` is nonempty in both modes. Dependency-free transactions need no proof wrapper; pruning away every dependency produces no wrapper.

**Mode 0 (Direct dependencies)**: When a mempool node receives a wrapper with `mode == 0`, they must verify that:

1. The `deps` list is the sorted, deduplicated union of all dependencies from each transaction in `transactions`
2. The total number of leanSPHINCS deps is `<= MAX_LEANSIG_DEPS_PER_DIRECT_WRAPPER`
3. The total number of leanSTARK deps is `<= MAX_LEANSTARK_DEPS_PER_DIRECT_WRAPPER`
4. `len(proofs) == len(deps)`, and each proof verifies its dependency individually

**Mode 1 (Recursive STARK)**: When a mempool node receives a wrapper with `mode == 1`, they must verify that:

1. The recursive STARK's public input `deps_hash` equals `get_deps_hash(deps)`
2. The recursive STARK is valid (proves all deps are valid)
3. The `deps` list is the sorted, deduplicated union of all dependencies from each transaction in `transactions`
4. The total number of deps is `<= MAX_DEPS_PER_AGGREGATE_WRAPPER`

Full wrapper validation in either mode requires resolving every transaction hash and checking the exact dependency union. An unresolved hash alone leaves the wrapper incomplete, not invalid. Nodes recover missing envelopes within finite byte and work budgets and a finite deadline, check each envelope against its claimed hash, and check the dependency union before or alongside proof verification. Until all entries are resolved and the wrapper is fully validated, it must not be used for admission, reaggregation or relay. Exhausting those bounds abandons recovery without establishing transaction or proof invalidity.

A node validates a wrapper under its own configured rules for the wrapper's target fork, the fork active at `head.timestamp + L`, the timestamp of the slot after the node's local head, even if that slot is missed. `L` is the current slot length, `(head.timestamp - parent.timestamp) // (head.slot_number - parent.slot_number)` (integer division, timestamps in seconds) for the head and its parent, with the [EIP-7843](./eip-7843.md) slot number. `L` is a local estimate for mempool validation only and does not bound any proof retention or availability rule. If the head has no parent or `head.slot_number <= parent.slot_number`, the target fork is the head's fork. Slot length can change, for example at a fork; a head and parent straddling the change can give a stale or averaged slot-length estimate, a misprediction the transport's fork grace window absorbs. The target fork fixes the verifier: `AGGREGATED_VK` for a mode-1 proof, and each scheme's verification parameters for mode-0 proofs. Peers do not negotiate verifiers, and a wrapper does not name one. When the local head advances to a target fork whose verifier differs from the one a held wrapper was validated under, the node drops every mode-1 wrapper, since a proof for the old `AGGREGATED_VK` can never verify under the new one, and keeps a mode-0 wrapper only if it re-validates under the new fork, which in practice requires its schemes' verification parameters to be unchanged; until then it does not use the wrapper for admission, reaggregation or relay. A transaction covered only by dropped wrappers is no longer proven, and re-aggregation needs the original mode-0 witnesses, which aggregates discard, so nodes should retain the mode-0 witnesses of transactions they hold for a bounded window before a scheduled key change and senders should rebroadcast their mode-0 wrappers after it; otherwise dependency-bearing transactions leave the mempool for the first slots after the fork.

Nodes should keep two bounded proof-verdict caches, because a mode-0 proof and a mode-1 proof are checked by different verification operations:

- direct-witness verdicts, keyed by `(dep, keccak256(witness), scheme_params)`, where `witness` is a mode-0 proof, `dep` the 96-byte encoding of the dependency it verifies, and `scheme_params` an identifier of the target fork's verification parameters for that dependency's scheme;
- aggregate-proof verdicts, keyed by `(keccak256(stark_proof), get_deps_hash(deps), AGGREGATED_VK)` for a mode-1 proof, with the target fork's `AGGREGATED_VK`.

A lookup consults only the cache for the operation it would run, and an entry applies only under the verifier in its key. A known proof arriving with a different or unresolvable transaction list under the same verifier is not verified again. A direct witness for one dependency placed in a one-dependency mode-1 wrapper, or any proof after the target fork's verifier changes, misses and is verified.

After validation, admission is evaluated per transaction: rejection for current state, fees or local pool policy does not invalidate the proof or require rejecting other transactions.

Each wrapper contains exactly one STARK in mode 1, and one STARK per leanSTARK dependency in mode 0. Nodes may maintain multiple bounded wrappers. `AGGREGATION_INTERVAL` is a recommended scheduling cadence, not a requirement to regenerate or broadcast the entire mempool on every tick.

Mode 0 is intended to be used primarily by clients broadcasting their transactions. If desired, mempool nodes whose local transaction pool contains ONLY leanSPHINCS signatures and no STARKs may choose to rebroadcast a mode 0 aggregation that naively concatenates the leanSPHINCS instead of proving them, as doing so may be more space-efficient within the mode-0 dependency limits.

The wrapper object is not a valid transaction on its own. Mempool nodes, FOCIL creators and builders use the **same recursive STARK format** to aggregate proofs. The only difference is the scope: mempool nodes aggregate over their current view of valid and includable transactions, while builders aggregate over the transactions they decide to include in the block.

### Recursive Mempool Aggregation

This EIP introduces **recursive aggregation** at the mempool layer. This allows mempool nodes to combine proofs and reduce bandwidth overhead to builders.

Mempool nodes follow the following algorithm:

- They passively wait and receive wrapper objects from users. They fully validate each wrapper as above, including transaction recovery, the dependency union and proofs, before evaluating admission per transaction.
- When a transaction becomes unincludable (either because some other transaction from the same sender is included onchain, or because of any other mempool expiry rule), they recompute `discard_dependencies` for the retained transactions
- Every `AGGREGATION_INTERVAL` milliseconds, they may generate new wrapper objects containing bounded subsets of still-valid transactions: using mode 0 or mode 1 for nonempty dependency unions. They forward useful updates with the same hash-only transaction list to every peer.

Splitting or pruning a wrapper uses `discard_dependencies` for each resulting transaction set and requires a proof matching its exact dependency union. Nodes may forward validated wrappers without reaggregation and retain older wrappers within local budgets.

For each batch, if a node anticipates that it will not have enough proving time to aggregate in _all_ aggregates that it saw, it should prioritize in the following order:

1. Its own aggregate from the previous round
2. Removing anything that needs to be removed
3. Other nodes' aggregates, in descending order of how many new transactions they will add to the aggregate
4. Users' transactions, in descending order of priority fee

Nodes may also alternate between adding one object at a time from (3) and (4).

#### Mempool-Level Limits

To prevent DoS attacks, `MAX_LEANSIG_DEPS_PER_DIRECT_WRAPPER` and `MAX_LEANSTARK_DEPS_PER_DIRECT_WRAPPER` apply only to mode 0; mode 1 uses `MAX_DEPS_PER_AGGREGATE_WRAPPER`. Every wrapper contains 1 to `MAX_TXS_PER_WRAPPER` transactions and is at most `MAX_WRAPPER_BYTES` bytes in the wrapper encoding above. `MAX_WRAPPER_BYTES` is the `eth` soft response limit, so the wrapper transport moves each wrapper as one whole object. It must be at least the encoded size of a mode-1 wrapper at the transaction and dependency ceilings carrying a `MAX_AGGREGATE_PROOF_BYTES` proof. At those ceilings the transaction-hash and dependency lists encode in 536,584 bytes, leaving 1,560,555 bytes (about 1.49 MiB) for the proof; at `MAX_AGGREGATE_PROOF_BYTES` the wrapper is 1,847,317 bytes. The gap between `MAX_AGGREGATE_PROOF_BYTES` and that remainder is headroom reserved for raising `MAX_TXS_PER_WRAPPER` or `MAX_DEPS_PER_AGGREGATE_WRAPPER`. If benchmarks put aggregate proofs above that remainder, the wrapper transport needs chunked retrieval; raising `MAX_WRAPPER_BYTES` alone is not enough.

Mode 1 verifies one aggregate proof, so its dependency-count bound is independent of scheme; the verifier's input and resource bounds apply separately. The activated proof format and `AGGREGATED_VK` must support every valid dependency set composed of enabled schemes within `MAX_DEPS_PER_AGGREGATE_WRAPPER`. A receiver whose verifier cannot verify such a wrapper refuses it locally and does not treat it as invalid.

Additionally, in both modes, two mempool-level limits apply to each transaction: a transaction can have `<= MAX_SIGS_PER_TX` leanSPHINCS dependencies and `<= MAX_STARKS_PER_TX` leanSTARK dependencies, added up across all frames.

These are mempool policy limits, not consensus bounds on blocks or inclusion lists. Dependency-count limits bound the final set, not private proof inputs or proving work, including work on discarded dependencies; the activated verifier's input and resource bounds apply separately. Scheme-specific limits do not enable a scheme. Counts and encoded lengths must be checked before allocation, and retention, recovery, verification and proving must stay within finite local budgets.

These limits in all cases apply to the length of the sorted, deduplicated dependency list. The list in a wrapper is required to be deduplicated in any case. For transactions, however, it does mean that it is legal to have a transaction with eg. `> MAX_SIGS_PER_TX` leanSPHINCS dependency _declarations_, if some of them are pointing to the same object so the deduplicated list is within limits. This behavior is wasteful but harmless; it is charged for and limited through the gas mechanism.

### FOCIL Compatibility

This EIP is designed to be compatible with [EIP-7805 (FOCIL)](./eip-7805.md), which implements fork-choice enforced inclusion lists. The compatibility considerations are as follows.

#### Extended FOCIL Data Structure

The FOCIL data structure is extended with a `recursive_stark` commitment, similar to a block header. The committed proof may be one an execution layer already holds from a mempool wrapper; an execution layer that does not hold it obtains it as a separate consensus-layer object, `il_proof`, like a blob sidecar:

```
[FOCIL]
FOCIL = [transactions, recursive_stark]
recursive_stark = [stark_proof_hash, deps_hash]
il_proof = [stark_proof, proof_deps]
SignedInclusionListProof = [slot, committee_index, stark_proof_hash, deps_hash, il_proof, signature]
```

- `transactions`: The list of transactions in the inclusion list.
- `recursive_stark`: A commitment to a recursive STARK whose dependency set contains the dependencies of all `transactions`, or null for no proof.
- `stark_proof_hash`: `keccak256(stark_proof)`, where `stark_proof` is the committed proof.
- `deps_hash`: `get_deps_hash(proof_deps)`, where `proof_deps` is the proof's sorted, deduplicated dependency list.
- `il_proof`: The committed proof with its dependency list, each entry in the 96-byte encoding, at most `MAX_DEPS_PER_AGGREGATE_WRAPPER` entries.
- `SignedInclusionListProof`: an `il_proof` published by the member at `committee_index` of `slot`'s IL committee. `signature` is that member's validator signature over `[slot, committee_index, stark_proof_hash, deps_hash]`, signed as EIP-7805 signs lists but under a new domain type distinct from `DOMAIN_IL_COMMITTEE`; the hashes bind `il_proof` as they do in `recursive_stark`. It is not a list commitment: the member's list may still commit to null or to a different proof.

The inclusion list signature covers `recursive_stark` together with `transactions`; `il_proof` is not part of the signed list, which commits only to its hashes. A member therefore publishes the `il_proof` apart from the list, in a `SignedInclusionListProof` on the proof topic (see [Proof Availability](#proof-availability)): a wrapper proof as soon as it decides to commit to it, from slot `N - 1`, the earliest the topic admits it (target: by `t=0s` of slot `N`, provisional); a reproven proof as soon as proving completes, during the construction window. A member has two topic publications per slot. It publishes the signed list during EIP-7805's construction window. Publishing early trades freshness for delivery: a proof selected before slot `N` cannot cover dependencies first received during it, and a member that changes its final proof to cover them, as it may, publishes the reproven proof on the topic as its second publication. Any committed proof is also served on request by the list's slot and `stark_proof_hash` as an `il_proof`, as blob sidecars are, as a fallback for nodes that do not hold it, whether they missed it on the topic or it was never published there: the consensus layer answers from `engine_getInclusionListProofV1` below. The signed hashes authenticate it from any source. Re-signing a list, including with a re-proved `stark_proof`, falls under EIP-7805's existing equivocation rule; this EIP defines no new equivocation.

A list's target block is the block it constrains, the block of the slot after the list's slot, and its target fork is that block's fork. The target fork fixes the `ENABLED_SCHEMES`, static validity rules and `AGGREGATED_VK` applied to the list; peers do not negotiate them. Let `deps` be the sorted, deduplicated union of `dependencies(tx)` over the transactions that decode and pass EIP-8141 and EIP-8288 static validity for the target fork; other transactions contribute no dependencies. A member commits null if `deps` is empty. A node holds a valid proof for a list's `recursive_stark` if and only if:

- `recursive_stark` is not null and `deps` is nonempty; and
- its execution layer holds a verified proof under `(stark_proof_hash, deps_hash)` and `set(deps) <= set(proof_deps)` for that proof's dependency list `proof_deps`.

The execution layer holds a verified proof under `(stark_proof_hash, deps_hash)` when it has a `stark_proof` hashing to `stark_proof_hash` with a `VALID` aggregate-proof verdict keyed `(stark_proof_hash, deps_hash, AGGREGATED_VK)` with the target fork's `AGGREGATED_VK` (see [Mempool Wrapper Object](#mempool-wrapper-object)), together with the preimage `proof_deps`, a sorted, deduplicated dependency list with `get_deps_hash(proof_deps) == deps_hash`. It obtains them from a fully validated mode-1 wrapper, whose `deps` is `proof_deps`, or from an `il_proof`. A verdict under another fork's `AGGREGATED_VK` does not count. A verdict without the proof bytes is not a held proof: the verdict cache only avoids verifying the same proof again, while a node that obligates a list's dependency-bearing transactions must be able to serve its proof. A committee member may commit to a mempool wrapper's proof covering its list, whether or not the wrapper is widely announced. A member SHOULD commit only to a proof it published on the proof topic. This is guidance, not a validity rule: a commitment to a proof never published there is judged like any other, but reaches only nodes that hold the proof or fetch it by request in time (see [Proof Availability](#proof-availability)). A member reproves only when no held proof covers its list, or to reduce surplus dependencies. An attester whose execution layer holds the committed proof checks the commitment with a cache lookup; one that does not must receive the `il_proof` on the proof topic, or fetch it, by the view freeze deadline or treat the commitment as null (see [Proof Availability](#proof-availability)). Only inclusion-list commitments may cover a superset; the block proof covers `dependencies(block)` exactly.

Null is the only encoding of no proof; an empty `stark_proof` is never a valid inclusion-list proof. A node that does not hold a valid proof for a list's commitment treats the commitment as null, whether the proof is unknown, fails verification, has `proof_deps` omitting a dependency in `deps`, or is committed for a list whose `deps` is empty. The list itself still counts: only EIP-7805 decides that, ignoring an equivocator's lists and not counting a list not received by the view freeze deadline. So a committee member cannot obligate dependency-bearing transactions through a commitment to an unavailable or invalid proof, or to one that does not cover them. An attester treats a commitment as null unless it holds a valid proof by the view freeze deadline, the availability assumption EIP-7805 already makes for the lists themselves.

##### Inclusion Enforcement

A list's dependency-free transactions are those that decode under the target fork and have no frame with mode `DEP_VERIFY_FRAME_MODE`. Every other transaction is dependency-bearing: a transaction with at least one dependency verification frame is dependency-bearing even if those frames carry no triples, so `dependencies(tx) == []` does not by itself make a transaction dependency-free. Static validity decides only what enters `deps`: a transaction whose dependency frame fails static validity or uses a scheme outside `ENABLED_SCHEMES` contributes nothing to `deps` but is not dependency-free, and EIP-7805 excuses its omission as an invalid transaction.

For each inclusion list counted by [EIP-7805](./eip-7805.md), a signed list from a non-equivocating member always obligates its dependency-free transactions; its dependency-bearing transactions are obligated if and only if the attester holds a valid proof for the commitment. Obligations remain subject to EIP-7805's inclusion conditions. An independent list can still require the same transaction. Holding wrapper proofs for a list's transactions does not obligate its dependency-bearing transactions when the attester treats the commitment as null.

Activation requires coordinated EIP-7805 consensus-layer and Engine API changes: signed ILs carry the commitment; the CL adds one signed gossip object, `SignedInclusionListProof`, with its signing domain, one req/resp request for an `il_proof` by slot and `stark_proof_hash`, and three Engine API methods below, `engine_verifyInclusionListProofV1`, `engine_getInclusionListProofV1` and `engine_submitInclusionListProofV1`; construction returns the list's transactions and its commitment, or null, for signing, with the `il_proof` when it is to be published, which the CL signs and publishes at once, publishing the signed list during the construction window; and block building and `engine_newPayload` receive each counted list as `(transactions, recursive_stark or null)` in committee-position order. Each CL, builder and attesters alike, passes a list's commitment only if `engine_verifyInclusionListProofV1` returned `VALID` for it on its own EL within its deadline in [Proof Availability](#proof-availability), and null otherwise; the EL obligates the list's dependency-bearing transactions only under a non-null commitment. The IL byte and proof-size bounds are separate, and interfaces must carry the per-committee proof volume bounded under Volume in [Proof Availability](#proof-availability). These changes need explicit CL agreement and new API versions; this EIP does not alter existing versions or omission claims.

`engine_verifyInclusionListProofV1(timestamp, transactions, recursive_stark, il_proof, attestation_deadline_ms)` takes the target block's `timestamp`, a list's `transactions`, its non-null `recursive_stark`, an `il_proof` or null, and `attestation_deadline_ms`, the EIP-7805 attestation deadline of the target block's slot as Unix time in milliseconds. The EL takes the target fork from `timestamp` by its fork schedule and applies that fork's static validity rules and `AGGREGATED_VK` throughout. It derives `deps` from `transactions` as above, ignores an `il_proof` whose `stark_proof` or `proof_deps` does not match `stark_proof_hash` or `deps_hash`, and returns:

- `INVALID` if `deps` is empty, since a non-null commitment is then invalid whatever proof it names; if `proof_deps` is known for `deps_hash` and omits a dependency in `deps`; if it holds an `INVALID` aggregate-proof verdict keyed `(stark_proof_hash, deps_hash, AGGREGATED_VK)` with the target fork's `AGGREGATED_VK`, whether or not an `il_proof` is supplied; or if a matching `il_proof` fails verification under the target fork's `AGGREGATED_VK`, or, without verification, fails the shape checks of `engine_submitInclusionListProofV1`; either way it records the `INVALID` verdict. After successful API parameter decoding, a supplied `il_proof` that matches the commitment but fails the proof-shape checks returns `INVALID`; a nonmatching proof is ignored.
- `VALID` if `deps` is nonempty, it holds a verified proof under `(stark_proof_hash, deps_hash)` for the target fork, as defined above, from a validated wrapper, an earlier `il_proof` or the supplied one, with `set(deps) <= set(proof_deps)`, and it retains the proof bytes and `proof_deps` until `attestation_deadline_ms`. When the EL already holds the proof, as for a committed wrapper proof or a proof submitted from the topic, this is a cache lookup with no proof verification. A supplied `il_proof` matching a cached `VALID` verdict is checked against the hashes and not verified again.
- `PROOF_UNKNOWN` if `deps` is nonempty, it does not hold the proof under the pair for the target fork, including when it holds a `VALID` verdict but no longer the proof bytes, and no matching `il_proof` was supplied. The CL may then fetch the `il_proof` and retry with it, within the deadlines of [Proof Availability](#proof-availability). This status is distinct from `SYNCING`: the execution layer lacks one proof, not the chain.

`INVALID` is final for the list, since the signed hashes fix the committed bytes and `proof_deps`: the CL stops fetching and treats the commitment as null, as it does for `PROOF_UNKNOWN` unresolved at its deadline. Either way the list stays counted and obligates its dependency-free transactions.

`engine_getInclusionListProofV1(timestamp, stark_proof_hash)` takes the target block's `timestamp` and returns an `il_proof` or null. If the EL holds the bytes of a proof hashing to `stark_proof_hash` with a `VALID` aggregate-proof verdict under the `AGGREGATED_VK` of the fork at `timestamp`, from a fully validated mode-1 wrapper or a held `il_proof`, it returns `[stark_proof, proof_deps]`, taking `proof_deps` from the wrapper's `deps` or the `il_proof`; otherwise it returns null.

`engine_submitInclusionListProofV1(timestamp, il_proof, view_freeze_deadline_ms)` takes the target block's `timestamp`, the `il_proof` of a `SignedInclusionListProof` received on the proof topic, and `view_freeze_deadline_ms`, the EIP-7805 view freeze deadline of the proof's slot as Unix time in milliseconds. The EL returns `INVALID`, without verifying, for a `stark_proof` longer than `MAX_AGGREGATE_PROOF_BYTES` or a `proof_deps` that is not sorted and deduplicated, exceeds `MAX_DEPS_PER_AGGREGATE_WRAPPER` entries or has a scheme outside `ENABLED_SCHEMES`. Otherwise it verifies `stark_proof` against `get_deps_hash(proof_deps)` under the `AGGREGATED_VK` of the fork at `timestamp`, returns `VALID` or `INVALID`, and records the verdict keyed `(keccak256(stark_proof), get_deps_hash(proof_deps), AGGREGATED_VK)`, with that fork's `AGGREGATED_VK`. On `VALID` it keeps the proof bytes and `proof_deps` with the verdict until at least `view_freeze_deadline_ms`, so a later `engine_verifyInclusionListProofV1` for a list committing to the pair under that fork is a cache lookup. A verdict held under the same key makes this call a cache lookup too.

##### Proof Availability

For the lists of slot `N`, whose target block is that of slot `N + 1`, the consensus and execution layers coordinate as below. The consensus layer, which knows slot timing, supplies the retention deadlines: `engine_submitInclusionListProofV1` carries the EIP-7805 view freeze deadline of the proof's slot, and `engine_verifyInclusionListProofV1` the attestation deadline of the target block's slot, each as Unix time in milliseconds. The execution layer derives no deadline from slot lengths. A retention requirement whose deadline has already passed imposes no retention.

- Proof topic: a consensus-layer gossip topic carries `SignedInclusionListProof` objects apart from lists. Its validation admits at most two proofs per committee member per slot, so topic traffic is at most `2 × IL_COMMITTEE_SIZE` (32) proofs per committee whether members are honest or not. A node forwards a proof only if:
  1. `slot` is the previous, current or next slot.
  2. `signature` is valid for the validator at `committee_index` of `slot`'s IL committee.
  3. `stark_proof` is at most `MAX_AGGREGATE_PROOF_BYTES` and `proof_deps` at most `MAX_DEPS_PER_AGGREGATE_WRAPPER` entries.
  4. `keccak256(il_proof.stark_proof) == stark_proof_hash` and `get_deps_hash(il_proof.proof_deps) == deps_hash`.
  5. It is the first or second proof from that member for `slot` to pass the checks above, a second having a `stark_proof_hash` distinct from the first's and arriving no earlier than `slot`. This admits the early wrapper proof and, if needed, a reproven one published when proving completes. A third proof, a repeated `stark_proof_hash` or an early second is dropped, which is not equivocation.
  6. `engine_submitInclusionListProofV1`, with the timestamp of the block of slot `slot + 1` and the view freeze deadline of `slot`, returns `VALID`.
- Requests: the requester is always a CL, using a req/resp request keyed by the list's slot and `stark_proof_hash`. The responding CL answers from `engine_getInclusionListProofV1` with the target block's timestamp and returns nothing when the call returns null. Requests are the unsigned, demand-driven fallback for a node that does not hold a committed proof, not a second publication channel. Topic publications are speculative and rule 5 bounds them; the signed list is the final commitment, and it authorizes retrieval and verification of exactly its committed proof, one per counted, non-equivocating member per slot. A CL requests only the `stark_proof_hash` that such a member's list commits to, and may always request it and verify it through `engine_verifyInclusionListProofV1` with the supplied `il_proof`, however many topic proofs it accepted from that member; a proof obtained by request does not count toward rule 5's two. A CL thus verifies at most three proofs per member per slot: two topic proofs and a committed proof that was neither.
- Authentication and equivocation: a CL fetches an `il_proof` or calls `engine_verifyInclusionListProofV1` only for a list that passed EIP-7805's P2P validation, which authenticates its signer as a committee member of slot `N`, and for at most one list per member, the first it received. A second, different list marks the member an equivocator under EIP-7805, whose lists are all ignored: the CL cancels queued and in-flight fetch and verification work for that member and starts none for its lists.
- Attesters: the CL calls `engine_verifyInclusionListProofV1` for each such list with a non-null commitment as it arrives, and on `PROOF_UNKNOWN` retries when the `il_proof` arrives on the proof topic or may request it from any peer, until the EIP-7805 view freeze deadline of slot `N`. Without a `VALID` result by that deadline it treats the commitment as null, and a later fetch does not change this. The design relies on the EL already holding the committed proof when the list arrives, from a mempool wrapper or from an `il_proof` published early on the proof topic and verified on receipt, whose delivery and verification during the slot the attester benchmark measures; the check at the list is then a cache lookup. Reliable delivery on these paths, up to the permitted volume, is an activation requirement. A cold fetch by request in that second need not succeed and will often miss; the list's dependency-bearing entries then go unenforced in that attester's view, while its dependency-free entries and other lists are unaffected.
- Builders: the builder's CL should obtain the committed proof of each counted list in its view. It may request `il_proof`s as soon as it receives a list and may continue after the view freeze deadline until the proposal deadline of slot `N + 1`. A builder without the proof by then passes null, still includes the list's dependency-free transactions, and can fail only against attesters that hold the proof, a liveness failure like failing to fetch a blob (see [Why does a missing proof drop only dependency-bearing obligations?](#why-does-a-missing-proof-drop-only-dependency-bearing-obligations)).
- Retention: an EL keeps the bytes and `proof_deps` of each proof `engine_verifyInclusionListProofV1` returned `VALID` for, servable until that call's `attestation_deadline_ms`, the end of the target block's retrieval window, after which slot-`N` lists are ignored. Evicting the wrapper the proof came from does not shorten this. A proof submitted from the proof topic, bytes and verdict with `proof_deps`, is retained until the submission's `view_freeze_deadline_ms`. At that deadline, slot `N`'s retention requirement is released for topic proofs not returned `VALID` for a list, and only those returned `VALID` are retained through the window. Releasing a proof's bytes need not drop its verdict, which spares a second verification if the proof is supplied again; without the bytes, `engine_verifyInclusionListProofV1` returns `PROOF_UNKNOWN` and `engine_getInclusionListProofV1` null. A proof is kept while any other retention requirement applies, such as a later slot's list committing to it; releasing one slot's requirement does not delete a proof another still needs. An execution layer may hold retained proofs on disk rather than in memory. Since the CL verifies at most one list per member, an EL retains at most `IL_COMMITTEE_SIZE` such proofs per slot, including an equivocator's: a proof verified before the member was found to equivocate stays within that member's share until the window ends, and its other lists' proofs are never fetched or verified.
- Volume: an `il_proof` is at most `MAX_AGGREGATE_PROOF_BYTES` of proof plus `MAX_DEPS_PER_AGGREGATE_WRAPPER` × 96 bytes of `proof_deps`, 1,310,720 + 393,216 = 1,703,936 bytes (1.625 MiB). The volumes below take both parts at their bounds; a proof well below `MAX_AGGREGATE_PROOF_BYTES` shrinks only the first, so near the dependency ceiling `proof_deps` can be most of an `il_proof`. A member may publish two proofs on the topic and its list may commit to a third, so a committee brings a node at most `3 × IL_COMMITTEE_SIZE` (48) distinct proofs: at most 32 on the topic and at most 16 committed proofs by request. Per committee (one per slot), a node thus verifies at most 48 aggregate proofs and receives at most 48 × 1.625 = 78 MiB. This bound is adversarial: honest members publish one proof each and occasionally a second, and a third arises only when a member's list commits to a proof it never published. A node already holding a proof from a wrapper checks it by cache lookup; wrapper proofs spread over the slots before the lists, reproven proofs arrive during the construction window, and a committed proof the node does not hold must be fetched, the cold case above. Storage peaks in slot `N + 1` before its attestation deadline, when three committees' proofs overlap: for slot `N`, the 16 committed proofs retained; for slot `N + 1`, 32 topic proofs and 16 committed proofs by request; for slot `N + 2`, 16 published early, since a member's second proof is admitted only from its slot on. That is 16 + 48 + 16 = 80 `il_proof`s or 130 MiB of FOCIL retention, since slot-`N`'s requirement for topic proofs no list committed to ends at its view freeze deadline. This bounds what this EIP requires a node to retain, independently of ordinary mempool caching of wrapper proofs.

Every attester that obligates a list's dependency-bearing transactions held its proof by the view freeze deadline and keeps it servable through the retrieval window, since `VALID` requires the proof bytes, not only a verdict, so a builder can fetch the proof from any of them whether it came from a wrapper or an `il_proof`, and its availability does not depend on builders holding the wrappers.

For a transaction imposing an inclusion obligation, omission is permitted only when EIP-7805 excuses it. Local mempool limits, proving time, proof retention or aggregation scheduling are not additional omission grounds. Per-wrapper limits do not apply to blocks or inclusion lists.

##### Builder Workflow with FOCIL

The builder's workflow in a FOCIL-enabled network is:

1. Receive authenticated FOCILs from the P2P network. Their commitments may name wrapper proofs the builder already holds; it receives any other committed proof on the proof topic or fetches it as an `il_proof` by slot and `stark_proof_hash`.
2. Receive mempool wrapper objects from the mempool. Each wrapper may contain either direct proofs or a recursive STARK.
3. Extract dependencies and validate proofs, including the commitment rules above.
4. Decide which transactions to include under [Inclusion Enforcement](#inclusion-enforcement).
5. Perform a recursive wrap over all included transactions' dependencies:
   - Collect all dependencies from all included transactions.
   - Collect all proofs from FOCILs and wrappers that cover included transactions (recursive STARKs from mode 1 wrappers and FOCILs, or direct proofs from mode 0 wrappers).
   - Set `discards = discard_dependencies(input_deps, dependencies(block))` as defined in [Transaction Dependencies](#transaction-dependencies), where `input_deps` is the union of dependencies covered by all input proofs, including mode-0 direct witnesses.
   - Generate a new recursive STARK proving the final set of dependencies; if that set is empty, use an empty proof.
6. Set the block header's `recursive_stark` field to `[keccak256(stark_proof), get_deps_hash(dependencies(block))]`.
7. Build the execution payload with the transactions and `stark_proof`, which is also carried in the block body.

Note that the job of a mempool actor aggregating to reforward, a FOCIL creator aggregating to generate a FOCIL, and a builder aggregating to include, are exactly the same task. Both take a set of wrapper objects (from mempool or FOCILs), extract their dependencies and proofs, and produce a recursive STARK covering the union of all valid dependencies.

#### Censorship Resistance

The recursive aggregation scheme preserves FOCIL's censorship resistance properties:

- Recursive proof aggregation operates on dependencies; wrapper validation still checks the resolved transactions.
- Recursive aggregation requires proving resources from mempool nodes and builders, which may introduce centralization pressures.
- Each mode-1 wrapper carries one recursive proof; total bandwidth depends on batch count, proof size, transaction data and dependency metadata.

#### Consensus Layer Integration

The recursive STARK scheme is compatible with the consensus layer's use of Lean Ethereum tooling. The same STARK backend and verification infrastructure can be used for both consensus layer signature aggregation and execution layer dependency verification. Inclusion lists (ILs) are just a different kind of package of transactions (like blocks) and use the same signature schemes as other components; no new signature scheme is required specifically for ILs.

### Lean Ethereum Integration

This EIP assumes the use of Lean Ethereum tooling for generating and verifying the recursive STARKs. This tooling can be found at the leanEthereum GitHub organization.

The recursive STARK is generated by:

1. Defining a Lean function that takes a flat list of `(scheme, data_hash, verification_key_hash)` triples and their corresponding witnesses.
2. Iterating over the list, and for each triple with `scheme == LEANSPHINCS_SCHEME`, calling `leanSPHINCS.verify(data_hash, verification_key_hash, witness)`.
3. For each triple with enabled `scheme == LEANSTARK_SCHEME`, calling `verify_dependency_stark(data_hash, verification_key_hash, witness)`.
4. Proving that all verifications return `true`.
5. Generating a STARK proof of this execution using the Lean Ethereum STARK backend.

The circuit is a relatively simple recursive "union and remove" construction, very similar to that which is planned to be used for consensus-layer signatures. It does not understand transactions or blocks; it only sees a flat list of dependencies and witnesses.

The verification key `AGGREGATED_VK` used to verify the recursive STARK is a fixed protocol-level value derived from the Lean Ethereum STARK circuit definition.

### Gas Accounting

The frame's `limits.execution` for a dependency verification frame is:

```
verification_gas(dep_verify_frame) = sum(
    LEANSPHINCS_VERIFICATION_GAS if triple.scheme == LEANSPHINCS_SCHEME else LEANSTARK_VERIFICATION_GAS
    for triple in dep_verify_frame.data_triples
)
```

Individual witnesses or existing recursive proofs are verified off-chain by aggregators. Validating nodes still verify the block's final recursive STARK when its dependency list is nonempty. The gas cost accounts for this aggregated verification model, rather than EVM verification of each individual witness.

Each transaction also pays for its share of the aggregated block proof:

```
recursive_stark_gas(tx) = LEANSTARK_VERIFICATION_GAS * len(dependency_triples(tx))
```

where `dependency_triples(tx)` is every triple in every dependency verification frame of `tx`, duplicates included. `recursive_stark_gas(tx)` is added to the transaction's intrinsic gas (`frame_tx_intrinsic_gas` in EIP-8141). It is execution gas, counts toward the transaction's `gas_used` and the block's `gas_used`, and is paid by the transaction's payer like the rest of the intrinsic gas. The block's `gas_used` includes no other charge for the `recursive_stark` field.

Because these dependencies are at the transaction body level and are not created inside the EVM, they are not affected by transaction execution reverting, and the gas is still fully charged even if the transaction reverts. This is similar to eg. intrinsic gas for calldata.

As a special case, notice that if a frame or a transaction has the same dependency multiple times, gas is charged for each declaration. So duplicate dependencies in a transaction are legal, but are wasteful.

## Rationale

### Why hash-only wrappers?

A single transaction-hash representation preserves object identity across peers and separates envelope retrieval from proof transport. The 2 MiB wrapper ceiling matches the `eth` soft response limit and holds 4,096 transaction hashes and 4,096 encoded dependencies, about 524 KiB, plus a `MAX_AGGREGATE_PROOF_BYTES` aggregate proof, with room for up to about 1.49 MiB. Aggregate proof size is therefore this EIP's open transport question: a larger proof needs chunked wrapper transport rather than whole-object messages. The ceiling is a safety bound, not a target batch size; envelope recovery and verifier work remain separately bounded.

### Why may inclusion-list proofs cover more than the list?

Committee members often draw their transactions from mempool wrappers. Committing to such a wrapper's proof needs no fresh proving on the committee's critical path; a member reproves only when no held proof covers its list, or to reduce surplus dependencies. The list commits only to the proof's hashes, so the proof travels apart from it: a member publishes it on the proof topic as soon as it decides to commit, and attesters verify it during the slot, leaving lists and cache lookups for the second before the view freeze deadline. A second publication covers a proof reproven for transactions first seen during the slot. Exact per-list proofs would instead have every member prove afresh and publish late in the window. Surplus dependencies under a commitment impose no obligation, since obligations follow from the list's transactions, and the builder discards them when it merges; the block proof stays exact.

### Why does a missing proof drop only dependency-bearing obligations?

An attester's obligation set for a list is either its dependency-free transactions or all of them, depending on whether it holds a valid proof by the view freeze deadline, and the first is a subset of the second. Obligation sets therefore differ when proof availability differs: attesters holding the proof require the list's dependency-bearing entries, while a builder without it does not include them and fails against those attesters. A block is judged against each attester's own view, as EIP-7805 already judges it against each attester's own set of lists. Monotonicity bounds the disagreement: a builder always includes the dependency-free entries, whatever proofs it holds, so every attester's required set is a subset of what a proof-holding builder includes, and views can differ only on dependency-bearing entries, the liveness failure of a missing proof. A member whose commitment is unavailable or invalid loses only its list's dependency-bearing entries; its dependency-free entries still bind.

### Why include the scheme in every dependency digest?

The uniform 96-byte encoding binds the verification scheme without special cases for homogeneous lists, at the cost of hashing more bytes than a 64-byte encoding.

### Why predict the target fork locally?

Wrapper validation estimates the next-slot fork from the local head. Around fork or slot-length changes, estimates may temporarily differ between peers. The transport's grace window avoids penalizing these differences, although wrapper propagation may be delayed. Inclusion-list proof checks use timestamps and retention deadlines supplied by the consensus layer.

### Why a new frame mode instead of an EVM opcode?

If an EVM opcode were chosen instead, the core question is how to handle missing or invalid proofs. From a mempool node's point of view, if it sees a dependency appear during transaction execution, and it has no valid proof for it, then it must be able to reject that transaction. And if a mempool accepts a transaction, it must be confident that EVM execution in later frames will not create _other_ dependencies that are unfulfilled.

The most realistic alternative would be for an opcode that creates a `(scheme, data_hash, verification_key_hash)` dependency to only be callable during an EIP-8141 validation frame. This would be viable. However, it would still make the mempool and builder's job relatively harder, because the EVM execution and dependency validation would have to be done sequentially rather than in parallel. It would also add more need to reason about special cases where EVM execution might add a dependency but then revert.

### Why recursive STARKs?

Recursive STARKs allow the aggregation of many individual proofs into a single proof. This is essential to avoid the size blowup of one STARK per transaction (that uses a STARK).

Each block carries a recursive STARK. This does increase the block size, a cost that has to be paid if blocks are to be verified without the individual signatures. The header holds a hash of the proof and not the proof: the block hash fixes the proof bytes either way, and checking `block_deps_hash` needs the block's transactions, so a header with the proof inside it proves nothing about the block that a header with the hash does not. Keeping the proof in the body makes header growth independent of proof size for readers that handle headers alone.

**This EIP proposes recursive aggregation at the mempool layer.** Mempool nodes generate recursive STARKs over bounded batches of valid wrapper objects they know about. This reduces bandwidth to the builder, as the builder receives pre-aggregated proofs from mempool nodes. The STARK-related overhead for the builder depends on batch count, proof size and update frequency.

The builder uses the exact same recursive STARK format to aggregate all included transactions' dependencies into the block-level recursive STARK.

### Why Lean Ethereum?

The Lean Ethereum tooling has already been extensively developed, has full formal verification in progress, and is the expected signature and recursive proof type for the future Ethereum consensus chain.

**This EIP's use case — processing many thousands of signatures and STARKs per slot without extreme data overhead — is almost identical to the consensus layer's use case.** The main differences are:

1. Hashes vs bitfields as identifiers of what objects are being proven
2. Many-time stateless signatures (leanSPHINCS), instead of the counter-based stateful signatures (leanXMSS) that the consensus layer can use because it needs to enforce one-signature-per-slot anyway.

Because the underlying use cases are similar, from a total spec simplification perspective, it is best to reuse the exact same tooling, rather than create a totally different set of tools for the execution layer.

Users who want to use quantum-resistant signature schemes other than leanSPHINCS signatures will have to either use them in the clear, and accept the onchain gas costs of doing so, or wrap them client-side in a STARK, and then reuse the leanSTARK route.

### Gas costs

As written, the EIP enforces fixed gas costs for both signatures and STARKs. This is assuming one parametrization of leanSPHINCS is used, and one parametrization of STARKs (or at least a small ratio between the average case and the upper bound).

A leanSPHINCS dependency costs 33,000 gas including its `recursive_stark_gas`, so in a quantum-signed transfer of about 21,000 + 33,000 gas the signature is about 60% of the cost. If the benchmarks in [Cryptographic parameters](#cryptographic-parameters) show the complete construction meeting the builder's deadline at the gas-derived dependency count, gas rather than proving is the binding constraint on dependencies per block. If they do not, proving binds, and the gas costs or the gas limit must change before activation.

An upper bound does need to be enforced. We make sure to add appropriate trace width limits and execution cycle count limits to STARK verification. In the current design (KoalaBear field, fixed trace width), this is enforced by the `2**24` trace length limit inherent to KoalaBear, and the trace width is fixed. If leanVM is redesigned, limits need to be kept in mind, so that the maximum possible proof size is a small multiple of the average case.

There may be two valid reasons to adjust the current fixed-cost approach and switch to variable cost:

- **Mixed k-time and unlimited-time usage of leanSPHINCS**: k-time signatures are significantly smaller than unlimited-time signatures, and SPHINCS could expose a parametrization that supports both. In this case, the former would need to have appropriately lower gas costs.
- **Different STARK sizes based on client-side proving time tradeoffs** Smaller proofs take longer to generate. We want larger proofs (cheaper to generate) to be possible, while still encouraging smaller proofs that put less load on the mempool nodes. This can be achieved by making gas cost proportional to proof size (or a related metric, like query count, and even polynomial degree).

### Why dependency frames may not sit in an atomic batch

Requiring `flags == 0` keeps a dependency verification frame from being a flagged batch member, but leaves it a valid terminator. [EIP-8141](./eip-8141.md) defines an atomic batch as a maximal run `[i, j]` where frames `i` through `j-1` carry `ATOMIC_BATCH_FLAG` and frame `j` does not, and its successor rule excludes only `VERIFY` frames.

The terminator is the frame at which the batch commits. When a batch fails its remaining frames are skipped and take receipt status `0x2`, but a dependency is declared at the transaction body level and, as [Gas Accounting](#gas-accounting) states, is unaffected by execution reverting and charged regardless. A dependency verification frame in that position would be skipped and binding on the block at once. EIP-8141 already restricts the batch flag by mode, so a mode rule here is consistent with it.

### Why the dependency frame still gets a receipt

[EIP-8141](./eip-8141.md) requires a frame receipt per frame, builds the transaction's logs by concatenating them in frame order, and exposes per-frame results through `FRAMEPARAM` `0x05`, `0x0A` and `0x0B`, which read by frame index. Omitting the entry for a frame that does not execute would renumber every later frame's results, on exactly the transactions [Transaction Execution Visibility](#transaction-execution-visibility) invites contracts to walk. EIP-8141's own rationale prices its per-frame cost as covering the receipt sub-entry that each frame adds.

## Backwards Compatibility

This EIP introduces a new frame mode, a header commitment and a block-body proof field. It does not modify the behavior of existing transactions. Transactions without signature or STARK verification frames are unaffected.

Nodes that do not implement this EIP will:

- Reject transactions containing `DEP_VERIFY_FRAME_MODE` frames as invalid (unknown frame mode).
- Reject blocks containing a `recursive_stark` header field as invalid.

This is a consensus-breaking change. Activation requires a network upgrade.

### Test Case 1: Simple leanSPHINCS Verification

A transaction with a single `DEP_VERIFY_FRAME_MODE` frame containing one `(LEANSPHINCS_SCHEME, 0, pubkey_hash)` triple, followed by a `VERIFY` frame that checks the existence of a dependency with a `pubkey_hash` pulled from its storage and a zero `data_hash`, which stands for the transaction's signature hash.

### Test Case 2: Multiple leanSTARK Dependencies

With `LEANSTARK_SCHEME` in `ENABLED_SCHEMES`, a transaction with a single `DEP_VERIFY_FRAME_MODE` frame containing multiple `(LEANSTARK_SCHEME, pub_input_hash, vk_hash)` triples, followed by a `VERIFY` frame that checks the existence of the dependencies with `APPROVE_EXECUTION_AND_PAYMENT` flags set.

### Test Case 3: Block with Mixed Transactions

A block containing:

- A normal EIP-8141 transaction.
- A transaction with dependency verification frames.
- A block header with a `recursive_stark` field.

### Test Case 4: Invalid Recursive STARK

A block with a wrong `block_deps_hash`, invalid body proof, or proof bytes that do not match the header commitment is rejected, including a nonempty proof when the dependency set is empty.

#### Client conformance

A dependency frame, wrapper `deps` entry or inclusion-list transaction dependency whose scheme is outside `ENABLED_SCHEMES` is invalid. Direct-witness tests reject noncanonical witnesses, a key whose hash differs from `vk_hash`, a proof for another `data_hash`, and exceeded leanSTARK verification parameter limits. Digest vectors cover both mixed and leanSPHINCS-only sets using 96-byte entries; equal data and key fields under different schemes produce different digests.

For blocks, mode-1 wrappers and inclusion lists, aggregate proofs at `MAX_AGGREGATE_PROOF_BYTES` pass the length check but still require full verification; proofs one byte longer are rejected before proof decoding or verification.

A proof for the former 64-byte statement cannot satisfy the new one, because the circuit and `AGGREGATED_VK` change with the encoding. It fails verification under the new key whatever digest is supplied, including `get_deps_hash([])`, which both encodings share. Changing `ENABLED_SCHEMES` likewise requires a new key.

#### Circuit conformance

These tests supply private inputs to the circuit. Clients receiving an aggregate check its public statement and proof; they do not inspect its private witnesses, nested dependency lists or discards.

Circuit tests reject unequal recursive-proof and dependency-list counts; disabled or unknown schemes in direct dependencies, nested lists or discards; and exceeded input, witness or verification-work bounds, including for dependencies later discarded. A recursive input above `MAX_AGGREGATE_PROOF_BYTES` is rejected before its verifier runs. For verified inputs `{a, b}`, discards `{a}` produce `{b}`, while discards `{a, c}` fail when `c` is absent, even if it is canonical and enabled.

### Test Case 5: Mode 0 Mempool Wrapper

A mode 0 mempool wrapper containing multiple transactions (including the transaction from Test Case 1). Verify that the wrapper's `deps` and `proofs` match the transactions' dependencies, and that each proof can be verified individually.

### Test Case 6: Mode 1 Mempool Wrapper

A mode 1 mempool wrapper containing multiple transactions (including the transaction from Test Case 2). Verify that the wrapper's recursive STARK proves all dependencies are valid, and that the `deps_hash` matches `get_deps_hash(deps)`.

### Test Case 7: Recursive Mempool Aggregation

A scenario where a mempool node receives multiple transactions and generates a mode 1 wrapper with a recursive STARK. Verify that the recursive STARK covers all dependencies of all transactions in the wrapper, and that the wrapper can be verified by other mempool nodes and builders.

### Test Case 8: Wrapper with Recursive STARK (Mode 1)

A mempool wrapper containing a transaction with dependencies, where the wrapper uses `mode == 1` and contains a pre-computed recursive STARK. Verify that the recursive STARK's public input `deps_hash` matches `get_deps_hash(deps)` and that the proof is valid.

### Test Case 9: FOCIL with Recursive STARK

A FOCIL containing transactions with dependencies, where the FOCIL includes a `recursive_stark` commitment. Verify that the committed proof, received separately, proves validity of all dependencies in the FOCIL's transactions, and that a builder can incorporate this FOCIL into a block-level recursive STARK.

### Test Case 11: FOCIL Compatibility

Verify that omissions satisfy EIP-7805's inclusion conditions and that discarding retains dependencies shared with included transactions:

- With sufficient block gas and valid proofs under the target fork's verifier, block dependency unions exceeding 4,096 signatures or 256 STARKs are not rejected by an additional dependency-count or weighted-budget check.
- Valid-block fixtures: blocks of transactions with one dependency each, as many as fit, are valid. With `o` the gas each fixture transaction uses besides its dependency, a block holds `floor(gas_limit / (33,000 + o))` leanSPHINCS transactions, or with `LEANSTARK_SCHEME` enabled `floor(gas_limit / (60,000 + o))` leanSTARK transactions. For `o` = 21,000, the transfer of [Gas costs](#gas-costs), that is 555, 1,111 and 1,851 leanSPHINCS dependencies at 30M, 60M and 100M gas limits, and 370, 740 and 1,234 leanSTARK dependencies. Fixtures recompute these counts from the `o` of the transactions they build.
- Builder benchmarks, not valid-block fixtures: in the gas-derived and cold inclusion-list cases of the [benchmark protocol](../assets/eip-8288/benchmarks.md), the reference construction discards every dependency outside `dependencies(block)` and produces the block proof within the builder's deadline, in however many steps it uses.
- Attester benchmark: at the required workload of the [benchmark protocol](../assets/eip-8288/benchmarks.md), wrapper proofs published on the topic by the `t=0s` target reach a cached verdict by the end of EIP-7805's construction window, and lists committing to them or to held wrapper proofs are checked by cache lookup by the view freeze deadline. Reproven second publications, the variant with transactions first seen during slot `N`, and the cold request case are reported, not required.
- A builder without a list's committed proof includes its dependency-free transactions; its block satisfies attesters treating the commitment as null and fails against proof-holding attesters only by omitting obligated dependency-bearing transactions.
- An inclusion-list proof covering a superset of a list's dependencies is merged into the block proof with the surplus discarded; the block's `block_deps_hash` covers exactly `dependencies(block)`.
- Per-wrapper limits do not limit the dependency union of a block assembled from several wrappers, or excuse omission of an otherwise required inclusion-list transaction.
- Local proving limits, proof retention and aggregation scheduling do not excuse omission, including when EIP-7805 evaluates transaction validity at an earlier index.

A block containing:

- Transactions from FOCIL inclusion lists.
- Transactions with dependency verification frames.
- A block header committing to the dependency digest and the valid proof carried in the body.
- Verification that the block satisfies FOCIL inclusion constraints.

### Test Case 12: Bounded Mempool Aggregation

With otherwise valid transactions and proofs, verify that:

- Two mode-0 wrappers with 16 distinct signature dependencies each can combine into a mode-1 wrapper with 32; mode 0 still rejects 32, and either mode rejects a transaction with 17 distinct signature dependencies for mempool admission.
- Mode-1 wrappers at the transaction, dependency and byte ceilings pass the corresponding limit checks; exceeding any ceiling is rejected. 4,096 total dependencies pass; 4,097 fail. With `LEANSTARK_SCHEME` in `ENABLED_SCHEMES`, passing sets include more than 16 distinct leanSTARK dependencies across transactions. Repeated declarations of the same dependency count once.
- For input dependencies `{a, b, c}` and retained dependencies `{b, c}`, `discard_dependencies` returns `{a}` even if a removed transaction also declared `b`. Retained dependencies absent from the inputs are rejected; changing the dependency set requires a matching proof. Dependencies covered only by mode-0 direct witnesses count as inputs.
- Either mode with empty `deps` is rejected. An empty transaction list, duplicate or unsorted hashes, a listed transaction without dependencies, a dependency frame with no triples, or a mode-0 wrapper whose proof count differs from its dependency count is rejected. Full envelopes are not wrapper entries.
- Verdict caches are separate per verification kind and verifier. With a cached valid mode-0 proof `w` for dependency `d`, a mode-1 wrapper over `[d]` whose `stark_proof` is `w` misses the aggregate-proof cache and is verified against `AGGREGATED_VK`; a cached mode-1 verdict likewise does not satisfy a mode-0 lookup for the same bytes. A known proof arriving with a different or unresolvable transaction list under the same verifier reuses its verdict. Once the local head reaches a target fork with a different `AGGREGATED_VK`, every held mode-1 wrapper is dropped and none is relayed after the transition; a mode-0 wrapper whose schemes' verification parameters are unchanged is kept, reusing its direct-witness verdicts.
- An unresolved hash leaves the wrapper incomplete even if its proof verifies. No admission, reaggregation or relay occurs until every envelope is resolved, hash-checked and the wrapper fully validated. Recovery expiry does not establish transaction or proof invalidity; a recovered dependency-union mismatch rejects the wrapper in either mode.

## Security Considerations

### Trust Assumptions

The security of this EIP relies on:

1. **Soundness of Lean Ethereum circuits**: The STARK circuits must correctly implement the verification logic.
2. **Soundness of the hash function used for `deps_hash` and `block_deps_hash`**: the fork fixes `DEPS_HASH`, and clients and the circuit hash the same scheme-bound dependency encoding.
3. **Soundness of recursive STARKs**: a STARK of a STARK of ... of a STARK must be sound. This is widely believed to be true and many constructions running live onchain already depend on this. However, this adds difficulty to end-to-end formally proving the scheme and its applications. Note also that the STARK verification keys that users put in to this system are up to users, and may potentially have unlimited recursion depth.

### Denial of Service

Aggregation work depends on proof inputs, overlap and discards, not only on the final dependency set. Gas charges for retained declarations do not account for all that work either. Activation therefore requires the complete construction to be benchmarked under the fork's gas limits and committee-wide inclusion-list input bounds; bounds on individual invocations alone do not bound total work.

Mempool per-transaction and wrapper limits are not consensus limits on blocks or inclusion lists. Mempool admission and transaction validity add these limits:

- `MAX_SIGS_PER_TX` limits the number of leanSPHINCS dependencies per transaction to 16.
- `MAX_STARKS_PER_TX` limits the number of leanSTARK dependencies per transaction to 1.
- `MAX_DEPENDENCIES_PER_FRAME` limits the number of dependencies per frame.
- `MAX_FRAMES` (from EIP-8141) limits the number of frames per transaction.
- `MAX_LEANSIG_DEPS_PER_DIRECT_WRAPPER` and `MAX_LEANSTARK_DEPS_PER_DIRECT_WRAPPER` limit mode-0 dependencies; `MAX_DEPS_PER_AGGREGATE_WRAPPER` limits mode-1 dependencies.
- `MAX_TXS_PER_WRAPPER` and `MAX_WRAPPER_BYTES` bound transaction count and serialized size in both modes.
- The gas cost for verification frames scales with the number of dependencies, making large dependency sets expensive.

Wrapper limits bound each object, while finite local retention and work budgets bound total mempool resource use. However, an increase in mempool DoS exposure still exists, because quantum-resistant signatures and proofs are much larger and more costly to verify than the present-day elliptic-curve-based versions of these primitives.

Serving committed inclusion-list proofs adds a storage requirement: an execution layer keeps every proof it returned `VALID` for, with its dependency list, servable until the attestation deadline the consensus layer supplied. A cached verdict alone never yields `VALID`, so every attester enforcing a list's dependency-bearing transactions can serve its proof. The adversarial per-committee traffic bound and the peak retention bound are given under Volume in [Proof Availability](#proof-availability); retention is in addition to ordinary mempool caching and may be on disk. Each committee member is a validator, so a misbehaving member can force at most three proof verifications per node per slot it serves; honest load is one, occasionally two.

These limits are inspired by the mempool policy framework of [ERC-7562](./eip-7562.md) (as used in EIP-8141).

### Verifier Changes

A fork that changes `AGGREGATED_VK` invalidates every mempool aggregate proof at once, a liveness gap for dependency-bearing transactions until they are re-proven (see [Mempool Wrapper Object](#mempool-wrapper-object)).
